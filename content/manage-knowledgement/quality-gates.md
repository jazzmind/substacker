# Manage Knowledgement Quality Gates

Use these gates before moving a Manage Knowledgement post from draft to publish.

## Gate 1: Thesis Clarity

A post passes when it can answer all of these in plain language:

- What is the human dilemma?
- What changed or intensified in the AI-native condition?
- What distinction does this essay make sharper?
- What practical judgment should the reader develop?
- What should the reader do differently after reading?

Fail conditions:

- The piece is mostly vibes, terminology, or abstraction.
- The essay could be summarized as "AI is changing everything."
- The practical center is unclear.
- The post depends on cleverness of the title rather than usefulness of the idea.

## Gate 2: Manage Knowledgement Fit

A post passes when it clearly advances at least one pillar:

- Ignorance as a Resource.
- Knowledge as Force.
- Wisdom as Metabolism.
- The Right Amount of Knowing.
- Shared Values Before Shared Knowledge.

Fail conditions:

- The post is really about note-taking, productivity, or AI tools with no deeper knowledge practice.
- The post treats access to information as the same thing as wisdom.
- The post says "knowledge management" but does not reverse it into values governing knowledge.

## Gate 3: Lived Specificity

A post passes when it includes at least one concrete situation where the idea changes behavior.

Useful examples:

- a family conversation;
- a workplace decision;
- a research loop;
- a publishing decision;
- a friendship or trust dilemma;
- an agent interaction that revealed or obscured judgment.

Fail conditions:

- The examples are generic or hypothetical in a way that could come from any AI essay.
- The author is absent from the piece.
- The reader cannot imagine using the idea this week.

## Gate 4: Source And Claim Discipline

A post passes when claims are categorized honestly:

- **Observed:** drawn from personal experience or direct use.
- **Argued:** philosophical or interpretive claim.
- **Sourced:** factual, historical, scientific, technical, or theological claim with support.
- **Speculative:** clearly framed as possibility, not fact.

Require QA Judge review when a post makes claims about:

- religion or theology;
- psychology or child development;
- medical, legal, financial, or safety matters;
- AI capabilities or societal effects;
- institutions, professions, or groups;
- historical causality.

Fail conditions:

- AI-generated research is treated as verification.
- A myth, religious story, or historical reference is overextended.
- A personal observation is written as universal truth.
- A provocative claim is unsupported.

## Gate 5: Artifact Usefulness

If the post includes a companion artifact, it passes only when the artifact has:

- a clear trigger;
- a bounded purpose;
- a repeatable process;
- a concrete output;
- anti-patterns;
- one reflective question or practice that develops the user, not just the agent.

Fail conditions:

- The artifact is just a prompt pack.
- The artifact would work for any generic self-help post.
- The skill produces output but does not train judgment.
- The essay does not need the artifact.
- The author has not used the artifact personally before publishing.

## Gate 6: Anti-Gimmick Review

Before publishing, ask:

- Would this essay still stand if the skill file did not exist?
- Would the skill still be useful if the essay were not being promoted?
- Is the AI integration deepening practice or just making the project look novel?
- Is the agent acting as a mirror, coach, or checklist rather than an authority?
- Is the reader free to benefit without installing anything?

Fail conditions:

- The skill is the hook and the essay is filler.
- The agent is portrayed as wiser than the person.
- The post overpromises transformation.
- The publication starts sounding like a software product launch.

## Gate 7: Promotion Integrity

Growth copy passes when it:

- preserves the seriousness of the idea;
- invites reader reflection or concrete examples;
- avoids outrage, guru language, and novelty bait;
- points to the practical dilemma, not merely the AI angle.

Fail conditions:

- "AI changes everything" framing.
- Engagement bait questions detached from the essay.
- Claims that the skill will make the reader wise.
- Promotional copy that attracts readers looking only for tools or hacks.

## Pre-Publish Checklist

```markdown
## Manage Knowledgement Pre-Publish Check

Post title:
Draft link or file:
Companion artifact:

- [ ] Thesis clarity gate passed.
- [ ] Manage Knowledgement fit gate passed.
- [ ] Lived specificity gate passed.
- [ ] Source and claim discipline gate passed.
- [ ] Artifact usefulness gate passed, if applicable.
- [ ] Anti-gimmick review passed.
- [ ] Promotion integrity gate passed.
- [ ] QA Judge reviewed high-risk claims.
- [ ] Founder Advisor reviewed personal coherence.
- [ ] Technology Advisor reviewed skill file, if applicable.
- [ ] Author personally used the artifact, if applicable.

Final concern before publishing:

Decision:
```

## Default Advisor Review Order

1. Founder Advisor reviews whether the author actually owns the claim.
2. Strategy Advisor reviews fit, audience, and sequence.
3. QA Judge reviews evidence and overclaiming.
4. Technology Advisor reviews the companion skill, if one exists.
5. Growth Advisor reviews promotion only after the first four reviews pass.

## What To Track After Publishing

Track qualitative response, not only subscriber growth:

- Did readers reply with better questions?
- Did anyone use the artifact?
- What confused readers?
- What examples did readers contribute?
- Did promotion attract the intended audience?
- Did the post sharpen or blur the publication vocabulary?
- Should the companion skill be revised?
